Why Nightlight

Bedtime should end in sleep, not in one more episode. Nightlight tells one story, in a voice your child already knows, that slows down on purpose: softer words, quieter scenes, and a last line where everyone is asleep.

Three picks in. One sleepy story out.

Choosing takes about a minute. Everything after that happens with the lights off.

  1. 1They choose the cast

    Whatever they pick becomes tonight’s characters. Tapping three toys is the whole interface.

  2. 2Nightlight writes it softly

    A new story each time, at the length you chose, reviewed for 3 to 8-year-olds before it plays.

  3. 3It’s read in your voice

    Press play and dim the lights. The story is read in the voice your child already falls asleep to.

Your voice

Your voice, even when you’re away.

Read a short script aloud once, on any evening. From then on, Nightlight reads every story in your voice: on work trips, on late shifts, and on the nights you’re simply too tired to do the voices.

The shape of a story

Built to wind down.

The adventure comes first. Then the scenes slow, the sentences get shorter, and the last page always ends with someone drifting off. No cliffhangers, no “what happens next?”

For parents

No ads. No chat. Nothing your child can wander into.

Nightlight is a storyteller, not a feed. This is exactly what it does and doesn’t do, in plain words.

  • No ads, anywhere. There are no adverts in the app and none inside the stories, on either plan.
  • No chat. Your child can’t message anyone, and nobody can message them. The only thing they choose is three toys.
  • Every story is reviewed before it plays. Each one is checked for age-appropriateness for 3 to 8-year-olds: no frightening themes, no unkind words, nothing unsafe to copy. If a story doesn’t pass, it isn’t played.
  • It always ends asleep. Stories run 5, 10 or 15 minutes and finish with the main character falling asleep, so there’s no “one more”.

The Fox, the Teapot and the Quiet Hill

Once, when the whole house had gone quiet, a small fox who could never quite sit still found a mint teapot and the moon waiting at the end of the bed.

“We’re going up the hill,” said the moon. “Only as far as the last lamp.”

So they went: the fox padding softly, the teapot clinking a little with every step, and the moon floating just above, lighting the path in silver.

· · ·

At the top of the hill the teapot poured three cups of warm milk. The fox drank slowly. The moon yawned, and the stars yawned too.

“Is it sleepy time?” asked the fox.

“Nearly,” said the moon. “Close your eyes, and I’ll count the stars for you. One… two… three…”

And by the time the moon reached ten, the fox was fast asleep.

What age is Nightlight for?

Children aged 3 to 8. Stories use simple words and gentle plots, and every one is reviewed for that age range before it plays.

Does my child need to read or type anything?

No. They tap three toys, someone presses play, and the story is read aloud.

How does the voice recording work?

You read a short script aloud once. After that, Nightlight reads every story in that voice.

Can my child talk to the app, or to other people?

No. There is no chat in Nightlight of any kind, with the app or with anyone else.

Are there ads or things to buy?

There are no ads on either plan. The only thing you can pay for is the Family plan, at £4.99 a month.

What’s the difference between Free and Family?

Free gives you three new stories a week. Family gives you a new story every night. Everything else is the same.
