Same street. Two lamps.

Nothing else changed between those two pictures. The difference is where the light goes, how blue it is, and whether it burns at full power all night.

The lamp we have

A bare globe

  • Light in every direction. Sideways into bedroom windows, up into the sky, and some of it onto the path.
  • Cold white. Blue-rich light scatters more in the air, so more of it comes back as glow.
  • Full power until morning. The same brightness at 03:00 as at rush hour.

The lamp we're asking for

Shielded, warm, dimmable

  1. Shielded. No light above the horizontal. The lamp lights the road, not the clouds.
  2. Warm, 3000 K or below. The colour of a kitchen bulb, not a stadium.
  3. Dimmable. Turned down in the small hours, when the streets are empty.

Skyglow

That orange isn't weather. It's our street lights, coming back down.

Light that leaves a lamp sideways or upward doesn't disappear. It hits air molecules, dust and moisture, and part of it scatters back down over the whole town as a glow. On a cloudy night the cloud base lights up as well, like a lampshade over the fjord.

Bluer light scatters more strongly than warm light, which is why the letter asks for warm lamps as well as shields. Brekkøy isn't alone: by one global estimate, the Milky Way is now hidden from more than a third of humanity.1

Footnote1. Falchi et al., “The new world atlas of artificial night sky brightness”, Science Advances, 2016. Back to the text

What we're not asking for.

  1. 01

    Dark streets

    Pavements stay lit. The light lands where people walk instead of spilling into the sky.

  2. 02

    Your own lights

    The letter is about street lights. Porches, shop windows and kitchen lamps are yours.

  3. 03

    A number we made up

    No costs, dates or savings appear here. Those are the council's to work out; we're asking it to start.

To the members of Brekkøy Council,

We ask the council to adopt a lighting ordinance so that Brekkøy's street lights are:

  1. Shielded, sending no light above the horizontal;
  2. Warm, at 3000 K or below;
  3. Dimmable, so they can be turned down when the streets are empty.

We are not asking for darker streets. We are asking for light on the ground, and the sky back above it.
