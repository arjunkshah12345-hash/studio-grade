Why plan it

Most bad days on the hill start in the car park. Late starts, guidebook timings and a forecast read on the drive. Switchback does the sums before you leave: how long the ground will take you, when the weather turns, and where the last sensible turn-round is.

A planned day, car to car. Every stop has a time, and one of them is the time to turn round.

Safety

Planning help, not a guarantee.

A plan made on Friday night can't see Saturday's ground. Switchback does the arithmetic; the judgement on the hill is still yours.

In an emergency

Call 999. Ask for Police, then Mountain Rescue.

No signal for a call? Register your phone for emergency texts before you go: text register to 999.

  1. It can be wrong. Forecasts change and paths wash out. If what you see disagrees with the plan, believe what you see.
  2. It doesn't know the ground today. Snow lying, a river in spate, a closed track: none of that reaches a plan in advance.
  3. Your phone is not your navigation. Carry a paper map and a compass, and know how to use them.
  4. In winter it flags, it doesn't decide. It marks avalanche-prone ground and links the avalanche report. Whether a slope is safe is your call.
  5. Turning back is part of the plan. Every route has a turn-back time. Using it is a good day, not a failed one.

Questions from the car park.

Ask a person

hello@switchback.scot

Route questions go to someone who walks these hills. Usually answered within a day.

Can I just follow the plan and not think?

No. It's planning help, not a guarantee. It does the timings and reads the forecast so you start the day with good numbers, but conditions on the hill decide, and so do you.

Where does the weather come from?

Public mountain forecasts for the summits you'll be on. Every plan prints the source and the time it was issued, so you can check the full forecast yourself on the morning.

Does it work without signal?

The route card and GPX file do: download them before you leave. Planning a new route needs a connection.

How does it know my pace?

Free plans start from Naismith's rule and the pace you pick. On Hill, give it GPX files from past walks and it adjusts for how you go on steep ground and on the way down.

Will it plan winter days?

Yes, with care. It shortens the day to the daylight, marks avalanche-prone slopes and links the avalanche report for the area. It won't tell you a slope is safe.

What happens after my three free routes?

Your routes stay yours. You can plan again next month, or move to Hill for £6 a month.
