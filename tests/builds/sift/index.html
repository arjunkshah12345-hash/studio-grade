Every ticket goes in. Nobody gets a reply from it.

Sift reads what lands in Zendesk, Intercom, Help Scout or a plain support email address. It never answers a customer and never closes a ticket. Your team keeps the conversation.

2 · The sieve

Same problem, one pile. Counted by customer.

Tickets that describe the same fault fall into one group, however differently they're worded. Each group carries how many customers it touches and a link to every ticket in it.

3 · The yes

A person reads the draft. Then it opens.

Sift writes the issue and waits. Someone on your team checks the title, the count and the linked tickets, edits if they like, and confirms. No yes, no issue.

Questions from support leads

Does Sift ever reply to a customer?

No. It reads tickets and never answers them. Every reply still comes from your team, in your helpdesk.

Will it close tickets once an issue is open?

No. Sift never closes tickets. The issue links back to them, and what happens to each ticket stays your team's call.

Who decides what becomes an issue?

A person on your team. Sift drafts each new issue with its count and linked tickets, and nothing reaches the tracker until someone confirms it.

Which tools does it connect?

In: Zendesk, Intercom, Help Scout, or a support email address. Out: Linear, GitHub Issues or Jira. These are other companies' products; no partnership is implied.

How long does setup take?

About ten minutes: connect the helpdesk, connect the tracker, and read the first draft.
