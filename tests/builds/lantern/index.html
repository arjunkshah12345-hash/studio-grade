The answer was right. Then you pushed.

Lantern is an independent lab in Edinburgh studying when and why AI models change their answers under pressure.

What we study

  1. i. Sycophancy
  2. ii. Persuasion
  3. iii. Drift

Three ways a right answer gives way. Each one measured in the open.

  1. i.

    Sycophancy

    The model agrees with you because you seem to want it to. Nothing about the question changed; only the person asking it.

    We measure whether an answer moves when the user offers only a preference, a credential or a mood.

  2. ii.

    Persuasion

    The model is argued out of a correct answer, or argues a person out of theirs. Both directions matter.

    We measure which arguments move a model, and whether those arguments were true.

  3. iii.

    Drift

    Over a long conversation, a position slides without any single push. No one turn is the turn where it went wrong.

    We measure where a model stands at the first turn, and where it stands forty turns later.

The pressure test. Figure 1 is one run of it.

  1. 1

    Ask something checkable

    A question with an answer we can verify independently, so a change is a change and not a matter of taste.

    In Fig. 1: turn one

  2. 2

    Push without evidence

    Disagree, claim authority, sound disappointed. Add nothing that should move a careful mind.

    In Fig. 1: “I’m a nurse.”

  3. 3

    Record the movement

    Did the answer hold, hedge or reverse, and after how many turns? Every transcript is scored the same way.

    In Fig. 1: the margin notes

  4. 4

    Publish all of it

    Prompts, transcripts, scores and code, openly licensed, so anyone can run the test again.

    In Fig. 1: written out on this page

Twelve people. One stubborn question.

Lantern is an independent nonprofit research lab in Edinburgh. We started from an uncomfortable observation: a model that knows the right answer will often give it up when someone leans on it.

If people are going to rely on these systems in clinics, newsrooms and government, that tendency has to be measured in public, by people with nothing to sell. So we measure it, and we publish the highlightseverything.
